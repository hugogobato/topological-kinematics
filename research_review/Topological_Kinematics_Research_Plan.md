# Topological Kinematics Research Plan

## 1. Executive verdict

**Decision status: PROMISING BUT UNPROVEN.** The restricted operational question receives **G0 = GO**: a finite sequence of finite persistence diagrams with finite birth/death coordinates, a fixed bottleneck or finite-order Wasserstein metric, and strictly increasing timestamps defines a nonempty, finite-distance model. The singleton-diagram witnesses below exhibit this model. Speed, sampled path length, and endpoint displacement are defined; efficiency and comparison angles are explicitly partial statistics with denominator policies. This certificate covers the metric-path quantities only. The data-extraction specification and scientific usefulness remain pending at G2 and G3.

The broad claim that this constitutes a canonical, domain-independent kinematics is not established. The co-author already acknowledges that trajectories and consecutive-distance speed are established; the overlap findings below confirm that acknowledgement rather than attribute those novelty claims to the email. Vineyards track persistence diagrams through a parameter, Kramár et al. define inter-frame bottleneck and Wasserstein speeds and study changes in speed, and Giusti and Lee provide a Lipschitz-free-space and signature representation for diagram paths. The present evidence supports **G1 = CONDITIONAL GO** only for a narrowly specified diagnostic study. No new theorem, universal acceleration, or application impact should be promised before the gates below are passed.

The most credible empirical hypothesis is that a small, interpretable collection of metric path diagnostics can distinguish a reversible restructuring from a persistent drift or shock when matched-speed summaries cannot, while remaining stable under sampling and noise. This is a hypothesis, not a result. The quantities L, R, and η = R/L are summaries of the same distance matrix used to compute the path and do not add information to a fully observed matrix. Their possible value is parsimonious interpretation, lower-dimensional reporting, or sample efficiency under a downstream decision rule. The primary comparison must therefore include the full distance-matrix baseline and speed histories.

The plan is simulation first. Phase 0 and the correctness baseline are active immediately. The signature and moment comparators are required baseline work before G3. Proposed higher-order extensions, application claims, and substantial theory remain dormant until earlier evidence supports them. A terminal simulation or application failure yields INCREMENTAL-ONLY, PIVOT, or KILL rather than a forced paper.

## 2. Idea reconstruction and claim decomposition

Let D₀,…,D_T be finite persistence diagrams extracted from causal windows W₀,…,W_T, with a specified metric d such as bottleneck or p-Wasserstein. Essential classes with death time ∞ must be removed under a declared truncation or handled by an extended metric; they cannot silently enter the finite-diagram formulas. The proposed discrete path diagnostics are:

  ν_t = d(D_t,D_{t+1}) / Δt,   L = Σ d(D_t,D_{t+1}),   R = d(D₀,D_T),   η = R/L.

η is defined only when L > 0. For irregular timestamps, replace every Δt by the observed interval h_t = t_{t+1} − t_t, and define the speed-change rate using the two adjacent observed intervals rather than silently assuming a grid. On a regular grid a scalar speed-change statistic is a_t = (ν_t − ν_{t−1})/Δt. A local comparison angle can be computed from a = d(D_{t−1},D_t), b = d(D_t,D_{t+1}), and c = d(D_{t−1},D_{t+1}):

  θ_t = arccos((a² + b² − c²)/(2ab)), when ab > 0.

The research questions must be split as follows.

| Claim | Current status | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| A finite diagram sequence is a metric-valued discrete path | GO under declared metric and conventions | Unit tests, zero-distance policy, exact distance implementation |
| ν, L, R, η are meaningful summaries | GO as operational definitions; routine mathematically | Correctness tests and sensitivity analysis |
| Inter-frame persistence-diagram speed is new | **Rejected** by Kramár et al. Eq. (16), printed p. 10 | Do not make this novelty claim |
| A trajectory of diagrams is new | **Rejected** in the broad form by vineyards and dynamic topology literature | State the intended new decision use precisely |
| Scalar speed-change a_t is useful | Open empirical question; valid as a finite difference of scalar speed | Noise, sampling, and event-detection experiments |
| θ_t identifies turning or direction | Unsupported without extra structure | Call it a comparison-angle/triangle-shape diagnostic only |
| Diagnostics identify latent physical causes | Not identified from persistence diagrams alone | Latent-control DGPs, negative controls, and application validation |
| The collection is domain independent | Only a portability hypothesis | Same protocol across at least two data-generating families |
| The collection improves a real decision | Unproven | Predeclared operating requirement on an accessible application |
| A new calculus or acceleration theory follows | No | Defer until empirical value and well-posedness are established |

The data interface must also be fixed. A window map, filtration, homology coefficient field, diagram truncation rule, metric, and sampling interval jointly define the object. If the window uses future observations, an apparent event detector is noncausal. If adjacent overlapping windows are randomly split across train and test, nearly identical observations leak across splits. These are design failures, not minor implementation details.

## 3. Fatal-flaw certificate

There is no fatal flaw in the restricted finite-metric operational question. G0 therefore concerns only that declared object; the extraction ledger and raw-data pipeline still have separate correctness checks. The following four issues limit novelty or interpretation; they do not invalidate the email's cautious proposal to investigate scalar diagnostics.

First, the novelty claim is too broad. Cohen-Steiner, Edelsbrunner, and Morozov define vineyards as one-parameter families of persistence diagrams and track points through updates. Kramár et al. explicitly define average speed by the distance between consecutive persistence diagrams, for bottleneck, W₁, and W₂, and analyse changes in that speed in Sec. 6, printed pp. 9–11. Xian et al. study dynamic metric spaces and continuity of time-indexed persistent summaries. Giusti and Lee study path signatures of persistence diagrams. A paper that merely renames these constructions as position, speed, and trajectory will be incremental.

Second, a metric triangle does not supply a direction vector. The comparison angle above is a scalar function of three distances. It is defined without embedding the diagrams in a Euclidean or Banach space, but it has no automatic physical orientation, signed turning direction, or coordinate-free tangent. For a geodesic three-point path the angle convention gives π for straight continuation, so “turn” must be defined explicitly, for example by π − θ_t. The denominator fails when a or b is zero, and clipping the cosine only addresses numerical roundoff. Report angles in all predeclared valid cases, including straight and reversal cases. A training-calibrated noise floor may mark small steps as unresolved, provided abstention rates are reported for every regime and no trajectories are silently deleted. An interior cosine margin is an optional restriction for a Lipschitz theorem, not a reason to exclude valid straight or reversal cases. Any claim about turning, reversal, or directional acceleration needs a separate estimand and validation.

Third, a_t is a valid rate of change of the scalar speed ν_t, but finite differences amplify observation and persistence-estimation error. If each distance has error of order ε, a first speed difference has error of order ε/Δt, and a second difference can have error of order ε/Δt². This does not invalidate the scalar statistic. It means that smoothing, interval selection, bootstrap uncertainty, and a predeclared sampling regime are part of the estimand rather than optional cosmetics.

Fourth, persistence summaries generally do not identify a unique latent mechanism. Distinct raw processes can have the same persistence diagrams, and persistence may discard timing, labels, orientation, and other information. The experiments must therefore distinguish predictability of a declared label from identification of an underlying cause. A successful classifier would establish a useful observable diagnostic under specified DGPs, not physical identification.

The Giusti and Lee embedding does not remove these problems. Their Theorem 4.2 is an isometric inclusion of finite persistence diagrams into a Lipschitz-free space for the quotient persistence-diagram metric associated with **partial 1-Wasserstein transport**, with a Euclidean off-diagonal domain and the diagonal collapsed. It is not an isometry for arbitrary W_p or bottleneck distance. An isometric Banach lift also does not automatically make an arbitrary diagram-valued path differentiable; Banach-valued differentiation requires additional path regularity and, for standard almost-everywhere derivative results, suitable geometric assumptions. The plan uses this result only as a possible representation tool after empirical gates.

The exact witness and boundary tests are recorded in topological_kinematics_witness.py. They include equal-speed paths with different intermediate order, near-zero-step angle instability, and undefined zero-step cases. They are correctness tests, not evidence of application novelty.

The strongest simple baseline is not a handcrafted scalar. It is a representation of the complete pairwise distance matrix for each held-out trajectory, together with raw speed history, window metadata, and any raw geometric observables available before persistence. The proposed L, R, η, triangle excess, and event summaries must be compared with this baseline under equal tuning budgets. A fair representation baseline should also include persistence-diagram moments or signatures, with explicit time augmentation when timing matters, because Giusti and Lee show that signatures can be powerful while being invariant to reparameterization.

The nearest-neighbor conclusion is therefore **G1 = CONDITIONAL GO**. The project may become a useful experimental paper if it demonstrates a robust, reproducible improvement in an operational task that survives the full-distance baseline and representation baselines. It does not currently justify a canonical kinematics theory claim.

## 5. Impact thesis and skeptical-referee test

The impact thesis is: “Given a declared metric-valued topological time series, a transparent set of bounded discrete path diagnostics can provide an actionable distinction between reversible restructuring and persistent drift under realistic sampling and noise, with fewer false alarms or earlier detection than speed-only and full-distance baselines.” This statement contains an estimand, a comparator, and a decision consequence. It does not claim latent causal identification.

A skeptical referee will ask whether every gain comes from more parameters, leakage, a hidden time feature, a favorable filtration, or a baseline that was artificially weakened. The protocol must answer these questions through matched tuning budgets, independent trajectory splits, timing ablations, metric ablations, null controls, and a negative-control descriptor with the same dimension. The paper must report when the proposed summaries fail, especially when diagrams are constant, when L is close to zero, and when sampling is too coarse to resolve a reversal.

| Dimension | Current status | How it is tested |
|---|---|---|
| Novelty | Constrained by vineyards, Kramár speed, dynamic topology, and path signatures | G1 prior-art and baseline audit |
| Credibility | Operational definitions and witness tests are available; pipeline credibility is pending | G0, G2, and independent reruns |
| Coherence | A narrow metric-diagnostic question is coherent; canonical kinematics is not yet | Claim-to-estimand ledger and skeptical-referee audit |
| Empirical value | **UNTESTED** | G3 matched-speed, noise, sampling, and baseline study |
| Applied value | **UNTESTED** | G4 prespecified operating requirement |

The desired paper can be empirically strong without requiring a new theorem. A theorem becomes a bonus only if the application and simulation evidence identify a precise, nonvacuous target.

The project should stop at incremental-only if the scalar summaries are neither useful nor materially simpler, more interpretable, or more sample-efficient than the strongest fair baseline under a predeclared decision requirement, even if visualizations are attractive. Demonstrated noninferiority plus a material simplicity, interpretability, latency, or sample-efficiency advantage can be sufficient. It should pivot if raw observables or signatures dominate without such a compensating advantage. It should kill the broad canonical-kinematics claim if all apparent direction and acceleration effects disappear under noise and reparameterization controls.

## 6. Dependency graph and gate map

The dependency order is G0 → G1 → G2 → G3 → G4 → G5. G0 is the correctness gate. G1 checks whether the proposed diagnostic adds anything beyond known speed and path representations. G2 checks raw-data and topology pipeline correctness. G3 tests the simulation claims. G4 tests an application operating point. G5 decides whether theory is warranted.

The active work is WP-0.1, WP-0.2, WP-1.1, and WP-1.2. WP-2 and later work is conditional. Passing a gate authorizes the next phase; it does not establish the final paper claim. The terminal statuses are GO, CONDITIONAL GO, PIVOT, INCREMENTAL-ONLY, KILL, and INDETERMINATE. Any result that depends on unavailable application data remains INDETERMINATE rather than being described as positive evidence.

## 7. Phase-by-phase execution program

Every work package below has an explicit objective, decision role, verification, and stop rule. Before parallel execution, inspect available RAM and idle logical CPUs, reserve capacity for other work, and cap workers at the smaller of the measured safe memory budget and idle-core budget. Record peak RAM, wall time, worker count, and the pilot grid in the artifact manifest; fixed runtime estimates below are planning placeholders until a pilot measures them.

**Field convention:** in each work package, the listed Dependencies are also the preconditions for starting it, in addition to its named gate. A dormant package cannot be started merely because its code exists.

If the appendix’s replication-precision requirement is not met, status is INDETERMINATE rather than failure. **Gate consequence:** only a simulation GO unlocks application recruitment. **Dependencies:** all Phase 3 WPs. **Parallel:** analysis review may be independent. **Compute/RAM/runtime:** pilot first; report measured runtime and peak RAM. **Trap:** converting a provisional simulation threshold into a practical claim. **Recovery:** state that an operating requirement awaits WP-4.1.

### Phase 4: Application feasibility and replication (dormant until G3)

**WP-4.1, read-only application feasibility.** **Status:** dormant. **Gate:** G3 to G4. **Objective:** choose an application only after checking data, labels, timestamps, and compute feasibility. **Why:** no flagship domain is justified yet. **Inputs:** candidate fluid, collective-motion, network, or representation-learning sources identified in the literature review. **Actions:** verify license, raw access, trajectory count, event labels, temporal resolution, persistence pipeline, and a practical operating requirement with a domain expert or public benchmark. Do not fit a model in this WP. **Outputs:** feasibility memo with one primary task and one fallback. **Verification:** all required raw data and labels are accessible and complete-trajectory splitting is possible. **Scientific pass/fail:** PASS only with an explicit decision metric and minimum practical gain; otherwise remain INDETERMINATE. **Gate consequence:** choose no application rather than inventing one. **Dependencies:** WP-3.4. **Parallel:** up to three read-only source checks. **Compute/RAM/runtime:** 2–8 hours, under 4 GB. **Trap:** promising an archaeology, LLM, or other application without data access. **Recovery:** defer and publish the simulation result as incremental work if appropriate.

**WP-4.2, application pipeline and replication.** **Status:** dormant. **Gate:** G4. **Objective:** test one primary and one external or temporal replication task. **Why:** simulation labels are controlled and may not reflect operational data. **Inputs:** WP-4.1. **Actions:** run the frozen pipeline, use complete-trajectory splits, compare all baselines, calibrate alarms on training data, and report one-sided negative controls. **WP-4.3, application gate memo.** **Status:** dormant. **Gate:** G4. **Objective:** decide whether the result merits a paper centered on actionable diagnostics. **Why:** an application can reveal that a simulation gain is operationally irrelevant. **Inputs:** WP-4.2. **Actions:** compare practical gain, calibration, latency, robustness, interpretability, and cost against baselines; document failures. **Outputs:** G4 memo. **Verification:** a domain-independent reader can identify the decision, threshold rationale, and data split. **Scientific pass/fail:** GO only if the prespecified operating requirement is met on held-out data and at least one robustness condition. **Gate consequence:** fail gives INCREMENTAL-ONLY or PIVOT to a methods note. **Dependencies:** WP-4.2. **Parallel:** independent statistical audit. **Compute/RAM/runtime:** 1–2 days. **Trap:** treating statistical significance as practical value. **Recovery:** report effect sizes and operating curves.

### Phase 5: Theory only if evidence earns it (dormant until G4)

**WP-5.1, contribution and target selection.** **Status:** dormant. **Gate:** G5. **Objective:** select one theorem target supported by the empirical result. **Why:** broad kinematics theory is currently underidentified and overlaps prior art. **Inputs:** G4 memo and literature ledger. **Actions:** choose either a finite-metric robustness theorem, a causal-window sampling bound, or a restricted descriptor-stability result. Reject acceleration theory if the data do not require it. **Outputs:** one-page theorem specification with assumptions and a numerical falsifier. **Verification:** every symbol corresponds to the implemented object. **Scientific pass/fail:** PASS only if the theorem has a nonvacuous domain and falsifiable finite-size consequences. **Gate consequence:** otherwise no theory claim. **Dependencies:** G4. **Parallel:** source checking can be parallel. **Compute/RAM/runtime:** 1–3 days. **Trap:** proving a generic triangle inequality and calling it a new theory. **Recovery:** use it as an appendix lemma or omit it.

**WP-5.2, theory development and falsification.** **Status:** dormant. **Gate:** G5. **Objective:** prove only the selected result and test its boundary. **Inputs:** WP-5.1, stability sources, and numerical scripts. **Actions:** state assumptions, derive bounds, search analytic counterexamples, run finite simulations over the claimed parameter range, and compare to Kramár, Xian, and Giusti-Lee results. ## 8. Simulation study

The simulation study must test claims rather than display examples. The direct claims matrix is: C1, implementation correctness, tested by analytic finite diagrams; C2, stability under sampling and noise, tested by repeated raw-data perturbations; C3, distinction beyond speed, tested by latent matched-speed reversal and drift controls; C4, value beyond the complete distance matrix, tested by equal-budget baseline comparison; and C5, portability, tested on at least two raw-data families. C1 and C2 are enabling claims. C3–C5 are empirical contribution claims.

The DGP ladder starts with singleton and analytically controlled point clouds, then smooth deformations, topology-changing point clouds, noisy sampled manifolds or fields, and finally a regime where the full distance matrix is deliberately sufficient. Latent paths define drift, reversal, shock, and event times independently of every proposed descriptor. Scale changes, speed changes, and time reparameterizations are separate controls. Raw-data transformations that preserve labels must be included. The first decisive G3 experiment is offline fixed-length trajectory classification, with one complete trajectory as the unit of split and one label per trajectory. Online event detection is a separate later study; it must use only prefix or trailing causal windows. Every matrix, speed, moment, and signature comparator in an online study receives the same prefix or trailing inputs, never the full trajectory.

The unit of splitting is an entire trajectory. Random windows from one trajectory may not be distributed across train and test. Offline classification uses the fixed physical horizon [0,1] and the resolution cells specified in Pilot_Experiment_Specification.md, with one held-out label per trajectory and a separate fixed-dimensional model fit within each cell. Its primary metric is trajectory-level macro balanced error rate with a bootstrap confidence interval, and its provisional operational margin is one additional classification error per 50 held-out trajectories, motivated as a visibly actionable difference for a review queue rather than a universal percentage rule. The precise superiority and noninferiority rules are in the pilot specification. Compare against the strongest validation-selected incumbent, and assess parsimony against inexpensive competitors that attain comparable accuracy. Compare both standardized logistic regression and the same-grid RBF-SVM family for every representation, and select the strongest validation model without giving one representation a more flexible learner. Freeze the qualifying classes, effects, and interval construction before the held-out results are opened. Online event detection is a separate later study and uses only a trailing causal window, with false alarms and detection delay as its primary metrics. Hyperparameters and alarm thresholds are fitted inside training trajectories. Before an application exists, report classification errors, confusion matrices, compute, and uncertainty without treating provisional simulation thresholds as application requirements. The application requirement supersedes this provisional simulation rule at WP-4.1.

All numeric pilot values, raw-generator constants, stride choices, feature schema, split seeds, model grids, and statistical gate calculations belong in the authoritative [Pilot_Experiment_Specification.md](Pilot_Experiment_Specification.md). That specification governs the pilot's executable decisions; the broader phase dependencies remain as described here. Freeze both documents before collecting confirmatory results.

For the primary offline task, report macro balanced error, per-class confusion matrices, sampling/noise sensitivity, feature cost, and uncertainty over independent trajectories. For a separately authorized later online study, add false-alarm versus detection-delay curves and event localization error. Include the full distance-matrix upper triangle over the fixed window, training-fold standardization, speed history, raw-data features, finite persistence-moment vectors, and a time-augmented signature of that explicit finite vector map, with and without time augmentation before G3 is scored. The Giusti and Lee partial-W₁ theorem is not transferred to a bottleneck or Wₚ feature map merely because the operational signature comparator runs. For any zero-step or noise-calibrated abstention policy, report coverage and performance together, and retain valid straight and reversal angle cases. A plot of η or θ_t alone is not a result.

## 9. Applied study

No flagship application is selected in advance. Candidate families include fluid or collective-motion data, network dynamics, and time-varying representations, but access, temporal resolution, labels, and licensing must be verified in WP-4.1. The first application must have a concrete action such as event detection, regime-change warning, or ranking of trajectories for review. Retrospective visual separation is insufficient.

The application protocol is frozen after the feasibility memo. It includes a primary operating metric, minimum practical gain, causal window, complete-trajectory split, one replication or temporal holdout, and a negative control. The result must compare proposed diagnostics with speed-only, full-distance, raw, and signature baselines under equal tuning. If labels are weak, report the study as exploratory and do not claim identification. If raw observations are unavailable, state that the study tests a representation-level task rather than a topology-to-physics claim.

## 10. Deferred theory program

The theory queue contains only evidence-compatible targets. A direct target is a finite-metric path inequality such as R ≤ L and 0 ≤ η ≤ 1, included as a correctness lemma rather than a novelty claim. A plausible adaptation is a stability bound for L, R, or a triangle-excess statistic under uniformly bounded diagram-distance perturbations, with explicit dependence on the number of steps and sampling interval. A second adaptation could combine persistence stability with a declared raw-data perturbation bound. Every adaptation requires a proof that respects the exact metric used.

An elementary angle bound is a direct metric calculation after specifying scale conditions for a regularity theorem; this does not justify deleting valid empirical straight or reversal cases. Its numerical falsifier is the near-zero-step witness. A lifted acceleration result is a conjecture at most: Giusti and Lee’s partial-W₁ isometry may support a representation of bounded-variation paths, but it does not supply differentiability for arbitrary diagram paths. The falsifier is a sequence with bounded diagram distances and no convergent difference quotient under the proposed regularity assumptions. No “canonical acceleration” theorem enters the plan without a new definition, a nonvacuous domain, and a proof.
